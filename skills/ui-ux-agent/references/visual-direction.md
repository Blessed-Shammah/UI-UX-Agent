# Visual direction that belongs to the product

## Translate taste into decisions

“Clean” usually means clear grouping, limited competing emphasis, readable type, and predictable controls. “Premium” can mean precision, confident typography, authentic materials, strong imagery, or exceptional density management. Establish which meaning serves this product. Whitespace alone does not make a product useful.

Choose a coherent direction across composition, color, type, imagery, density, and motion. Keep a brief rationale for each. A familiar font or blue accent can be correct; forcing novelty into every element damages usability. Distinctiveness should come from the subject and composition, not decorative randomness.

## Composition and hierarchy

- Give each screen a primary task. Use position, contrast, scale, and space to communicate priority.
- Group by relationship; put a label nearer its control than the next field. Use a spacing rhythm such as 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, adjusted for platform and density.
- Choose a content width and grid. Long-form reading, side-by-side comparison, and dense operations need different widths. Avoid making every section a rounded card.
- Use realistic long names, missing values, large amounts, translated labels, and long headings early. Line breaks are part of the composition.
- A marketing page should reveal the actual product and answer buyer questions. An application should foreground work. Do not turn every product into a landing page.

## Color

Define semantic roles: canvas, surface, elevated surface, primary text, secondary text, border, action, action-hover, focus, success, warning, danger. A brand accent and error color should not be indistinguishable. Separate selection from hover and disabled from muted content.

Start with a restrained neutral family derived from the product's tone. Choose accent saturation relative to the surface and usage area. Large saturated backgrounds carry more visual weight than small indicators. Validate actual foreground/background pairs, including overlays and dark mode. Light and dark themes need independent tuning, not automatic inversion.

## Typography

Pick type for language coverage, readability, licensing, loading cost, and character. One strong family may cover an application; a distinctive display face can support editorial work. More fonts do not imply more craft. Use explicit roles: display, heading, body, label, data. Give each size, weight, line height, and tracking. Reserve expressive tracking for short display text; avoid cramped body copy.

For web prose, roughly 45–75 characters per line and a body size near 16px are starting heuristics, not universal requirements. Data-heavy desktop tools need a different balance. Check numerals and tabular alignment for money and metrics. Test fallback fonts and loading shifts.

## Imagery and icons

Use imagery that explains the offering or creates a relevant atmosphere. Prefer authentic product views and consistent art direction. Define subject, crop, lighting, palette, aspect ratio, resolution, and rights before sourcing. Generated images are optional and need inspection; avoid generated interface screenshots as substitutes for usable controls.

Use one compatible icon family with consistent optical size and stroke. Label ambiguous actions. Never use fake logos, fabricated customers, or unlicensed portfolio imagery as credibility. Decorative images get empty alt text; meaningful images get descriptions of their purpose.

## Motion and polish

Motion should reveal causality, state, or continuity. Start with brief feedback around 120–200ms and larger transitions around 200–350ms, then tune to distance and platform. These are heuristics. Prefer transform/opacity for browser animations. Support reduced motion; avoid hiding essential content behind entrance effects.

Polish the seams: alignment, baseline, truncation, optical icon position, hover/focus parity, loading continuity, and consistent edges. Remove decoration that competes with the task. Keep a distinctive motif only where it strengthens the product's identity.
